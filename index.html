Statistical and Cryptographic Properties of Q = kG on secp256k1

In the finite field 𝔽p of secp256k1, the x and y coordinates of generated points Q = kG exhibit an apparent uniformity across the interval [0, p − 1]. Because the generator G creates a prime-order cyclic subgroup of size n ≈ p, the distribution of x and y coordinates over the full cycle is nearly flat. Specifically, regarding the frequency of individual values, any valid x coordinate appears at most twice (corresponding to +y and −y mod p), and roughly half of the elements in 𝔽p are valid x coordinates (when x3 + 7 is a quadratic residue modulo p). Consequently, the distribution of bits within Qx and Qy approaches a Bernoulli(0.5) process, showing balanced 0s and 1s without local clustering. Furthermore, incrementing the scalar k → k + 1 causes a nonlinear modular inversion during point addition, destroying linear correlations between input and output and producing a strong avalanche-like behaviour where flipping a single bit in k flips approximately 50% of the bits in Q.

However, it is critical to distinguish between properties required for a cryptographic one-way function (like Elliptic Curve scalar multiplication) and those strictly required for cryptographic hash functions. For Q = kG to act as a secure one-way trapdoor, the essential property is algebraic: the computational intractability of the Elliptic Curve Discrete Logarithm Problem (ECDLP) and the absence of exploitable input-output correlations that would allow recovering k from Q faster than Ο(√n). Conversely, bit-level uniformity, strict avalanche criteria, and flat value frequencies are core design metrics of cryptographic hash functions (e.g., SHA-256) to guarantee preimage and collision resistance on unstructured bitstrings. In ECC, apparent uniformity and avalanche effects are statistical byproducts of modular inversion over a large prime field, not the source of security itself—which is why raw EC coordinates should still be passed through a Key Derivation Function (KDF) or hash function before being used as symmetric keys.
